My area of work: LND

  • I run it

    Ye₿ente, my open-source wallet, runs on my own LND node. I meet its invoices, payments and channels every day.

  • It's Go

    158 Go challenges at Zone01 Kisumu, and 100+ Go developers at LakeHub to learn with.

  • It's Lightning

    Fast, cheap payments: what people around me need to use Bitcoin day to day.

Alongside: reviewing Bitcoin Core pull requests, to learn how careful review is done.

The problem

In Kenya and Ethiopia, people pay with M-Pesa and Telebirr. Lightning can serve them, but the node software has to stay simple to run, safe for the people relying on it, and understood by developers close to the people who use it.

What I've done

  • 28 merged pull requests and counting on public projects, every one linked.
  • Ye₿ente: a Lightning wallet on my own LND node, with M-Pesa payouts.
  • Bitcoin Core Lab: 13 hands-on stages, regtest to mainnet.
  • My first talk at BitDevs Kisumu, now a free setup guide.
  • BitDevs Kisumu, which I co-founded, sponsored by Btrust.

Six months

  1. Month 1Learn the projectBuild LND from source; run the unit tests. Follow the code contribution guidelines. Review two open pull requests, saying exactly what I tested.
  2. Month 2First contributionTake a good first issue. Open a small, tested pull request. Answer review until it's merged or closed.
  3. Months 3–4Where Ye₿ente livesReview and contribute around the parts Ye₿ente relies on every day: invoices, payments and macaroons. Two more pull requests, with time left for review.
  4. Months 5–6Sustain and shareRegular reviews each week. Write up what I learned, in plain words, and present it at BitDevs Kisumu.

Each step ticks on my tracker when GitHub shows it. No AI: I write, test and review everything myself.

Who benefits

  • People using Ye₿ente: a node run by someone who knows its code.
  • Developers at BitDevs Kisumu and LakeHub: a path into Lightning, from someone a step ahead.
  • LND: another careful reviewer and tester, close to its users in East Africa.
