# Kisumu app research: the problem, the choice, and why

Date: 6 Nov 2026. Public research for a new Altradits page for Kisumu.
Nothing here is legal advice. Figures marked "could not verify" need a
check before anyone repeats them in public.

## The choice, in short

**Problem:** in Kisumu, Bitcoin has not started. Nobody holds sats, so no
local customer can spend them, and buying first sats with scarce shillings
is a hard ask when M-Pesa Kadogo already moves small amounts free. Every
circular economy that works started with EARN, not buy: El Zonte paid
youth in bitcoin for community work, Kibera grew out of a waste program
where residents earned, Ekasi pays salaries and class rewards in sats.

**App:** Kazi board at /kazi. A moderated noticeboard of small paid tasks
for Kisumu people. Anyone can post a task (a shop, a student group, the
diaspora, a remote bitcoiner). A Kisumu worker does it. The poster pays
the worker directly, peer to peer, in sats to the worker's own wallet or
in M-Pesa. Altradits never holds money, never takes a cut, never converts
anything. Earn first, then spend at kTown pilot shops: that closes a
Kisumu loop from what Altradits already has.

**Why this one:** it serves students and youth who need income (Maseno
alone hosts 20,000 students; youth unemployment at 20 to 24 is 16.8%),
it pulls outside money INTO Kisumu (diaspora and remote posters pay
Kisumu workers), it needs no licence-shaped custody, it needs no new
dependency, and Stanley can seed it himself by posting and paying the
first small tasks. Runner-up was a bring-your-own-wallet till for shops
that keep sats; it waits, because in Kisumu today there are no such
shops and no customers holding sats, and it would split Stanley's
shop-visit time with the kTown pilot.

## 1. Kisumu: economy and daily money flows

Kenya's third city, on the Winam Gulf of Lake Victoria. Luo-majority,
county government seat, lakeside trade hub reaching Uganda.

### Fishing and fish traders

Fishing is the signature livelihood. Dunga Beach, just south of the
city, is a longtime fishing village; Usoma Beach's main activity is
fishing too. The catch is tilapia (ngege), the small sardine-like omena,
and Nile perch. Omena boats go out on moonless nights with lamps; in the
morning much of the catch passes to women, who dry it in the sun.
Kisumu is one of omena's great markets. A 2026 Bergen study of Kisumu
market outlets found omena remains the most consumed fish in many homes,
above all among younger and lower-income households, because it is
cheaper, familiar and quick to cook; farmed tilapia is available but has
not replaced it.

Sources: [Macleki on Usoma Beach](https://macleki.org/stories/usoma-beach-in-kisumu/),
[Macleki on Dunga Beach](https://macleki.org/stories/fishing-at-dunga-beach-on-lake-victoria/),
[UiB omena and tilapia study 2026](https://www.uib.no/node/180936),
[African Centre for Cities Kisumu food paper (PDF)](https://www.africancentreforcities.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/AFL-FOODChronicles-3-Kisumu_30.03.2026.pdf).

Money shape: daily cash at beaches and markets, thin margins, women
strong in processing and trade. Could not verify: daily volumes, beach
prices, or how much trade already passes through M-Pesa.

### Kibuye, Jubilee, and the markets

Kibuye is one of East Africa's largest open-air markets; one closure
report put its usual load above 10,000 traders. Older county material
credits Kibuye with 1,000+ and Jubilee with 700+ traders; a 2026 county
plan for a KSh 3.5 billion Kibuye rebuild speaks of thousands of
traders across the Lake Victoria basin and Uganda. A Kisumu policy
brief counts Kibuye, Jubilee, Obunga, Kondele and Mamboleo as home to
thousands of women and youth MSMEs. A JOOUST study of Kibuye found
84.5% of SME activity is by people aged 35 and below.

Sources: [SNV Kisumu policy brief (PDF)](https://admin.snv.org/assets/KISUMU%20County%20Policy%20Brief.pdf),
[Kibuye closure report](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Traders+left+struggling+after+Kibuye+Market+closes+doors.-a0837444876),
[KSh 3.5B Kibuye plan](https://peopledaily.digital/news/ksh3-5b-kibuye-market-makeover-kisumu-unveils-plan-for-mega-trading-hub),
[JOOUST Kibuye SME study](http://ir.jooust.ac.ke/handle/123456789/2980),
[Kenya markets investment](https://www.aipdf.org/kenya-to-invest-us-9-9m-in-modern-markets/).

Money shape: market-day cash and M-Pesa, stock bought each morning,
savings in chama groups (below). Could not verify: current trader
counts per market (figures span years and disagree).

### Boda boda and matatu

Kisumu and Nakuru have unusually high bicycle boda shares (16 to 20% of
weekday trips in a 500-rider survey). Motorbike bodas dominate growth
nationally: NTSA counted nearly 1.4 million registered motorcycles by
2018, and the sector is credited with about 3.4% of GDP and over a
million direct livelihoods (secondary press summary; could not verify
against the primary report). In Kisumu, riders keep training and
organising: 400+ riders took free road-safety training in 2025.

Sources: [Kisumu and Nakuru boda survey (PDF)](https://repository.up.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/1c3b24bb-f73b-4e8f-bb7d-e088e642e369/content),
[Kenya boda sector overview](https://phoenix-browser.com/?d=4295713009812117480),
[400 Kisumu riders trained](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/?p=103313),
[UNDP Kisumu bicycle taxis 2000](https://www.ibike.org/pabin/articles/kn_boda3.htm).

Money shape: daily cash earnings, fuel and loan payments daily, phone
always in hand. Could not verify: current Kisumu rider count.

### Jua kali

Jua kali sheds sit across Kisumu's estates, from Kondele to Nyalenda to
Obunga and Dunga: furniture makers, tailors, welders (gates, fences),
mechanics, metal workers, artists. The county has a registered Jua Kali
Artisans Association with its own Kisumu land case in court. MPs keep
promising industrial-park upgrades; artisans complain of stalled
support.

Sources: [Macleki on jua kali in Kisumu](https://macleki.org/stories/jua-kali-in-kisumu/),
[Kisumu jua kali court case](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/keelc/2025/7905/eng@2025-11-11),
[Kisumu jua kali policy debate](https://radiogeneration.co.ke/kisumu-east-mp-shabbir-faults-state-over-stalled-growth-of-jua-kali-sector_33804).

Money shape: job-by-job payments, deposits for materials, repeat local
customers. Could not verify: artisan counts or earnings in Kisumu.

### Chamas and savings groups

Kenya's grassroots banks. Widely cited figures: about 300,000 chamas
holding about KSh 300 billion, one in three Kenyans a member (old
figures, still repeated; treat as order-of-magnitude, could not verify
against a fresh primary source). Newer: women are about 97% of chama
members, and by mid-2023 almost half of Kenyan women were in one.
Typical group: 10 to 35 members, weekly or monthly contributions,
merry-go-round payouts or table banking with small loans.

Sources: [Chama investment overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chama_(investment)),
[Women and youth financial inclusion](https://rbc.co.ke/expanding-opportunities-women-and-youth-driving-financial-inclusion-in-kenya/),
[Chamas explained](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/understanding-chamas-kenya-investment-groups-merry-go-rounds-collective-saving).

Money shape: pooled cash and M-Pesa, payout discipline by peer trust.
No bitcoin path exists in any chama tool found (eChama, Kusanya and
Stacked+ are fiat record keepers).

### Students

Maseno University hosts 20,000+ students across its campuses including
Kisumu City Campus in the city centre (business, arts, graduate
programmes). Youth unemployment (KNBS 2025 Economic Survey, via press):
16.8% at ages 20 to 24, 11.1% at 15 to 19. Over 80% of new labour
market entrants land in the informal sector; about 87% of the 822,100
jobs created in 2025 were informal. Wider measures are darker: the
Federation of Kenya Employers puts 67% of youth 15 to 34 as unemployed,
underemployed or inactive, and the World Bank counts only 8.6% of
employed youth in formal jobs.

Sources: [Maseno campuses](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/maseno-university-equator-campus-western-kenya-education),
[KNBS youth unemployment via People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/inside-politics/tuju-warns-kenya-faces-instability-over-unemployment-corruption-and-opulence),
[822,100 jobs, 87% informal](https://peopledaily.digital/insights/youth-unemployment-is-a-ticking-time-bomb-amid-hope-over-vision-2060-plan),
[8.6% formal youth jobs](https://peopledaily.digital/insights/youth-unemployment-inside-the-8-6-trap-kenyas-graduates-cannot-escape).

Money shape: upkeep money from home, side hustles, phone-first,
data-poor. Students are the natural first earners of task sats.

### M-Pesa use

M-Pesa is the rails everything else must beat. Safaricom's FY26
(year to March 2026): 46.4 billion transactions worth KSh 41.68
trillion, 41.7 million active customers. The detail that matters for
Bitcoin: 17.1 billion of those transactions were free under M-Pesa
Kadogo (person-to-person up to KSh 100, merchant payments under KSh
200, deposits, airtime). Small amounts in Kenya already move free.
Bitcoin wins on what M-Pesa cannot do (receive from abroad without an
agent, hold outside the shilling, permissionless earning), not on
small-payment fees.

Sources: [46.4B transactions FY26](https://peopledaily.digital/business/safaricom-reports-46-4b-m-pesa-transactions-in-one-year),
[17.1B free Kadogo transactions](https://techweez.com/2026/05/15/m-pesa-kadogo-transactions-growth/),
[40M users March 2026](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/m-pesa-40-million-users-kenya-marks-19-years-mobile-money).

## 2. The Bitcoin ecosystem relevant to this

### Circular economies: what worked

**Bitcoin Beach, El Zonte (El Salvador).** The original. From 2019 an
anonymous bitcoin donation funded a circular economy in a town of about
3,000: teenagers paid in bitcoin as lifeguards and for beach and river
cleanups, English classes, local shops taking bitcoin for basics. Work
first, spending loop second. It inspired the 2021 legal-tender law.

Sources: [Lookout on Bitcoin Beach](https://lookout.co/welcome-to-bitcoin-beach-how-a-california-surfer-brought-cryptocurrency-to-a-small-village-in-el-salvador),
[Bitcoin Beach empowers](https://bitcoinnews.com/p/bitcoin-beach-roman-martinez),
[Nasdaq 2020](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/on-the-coast-of-el-salvador-bitcoin-is-becoming-the-standard-2020-01-13).

**AfriBit Kibera (Nairobi).** Closest model to Kisumu. A non-profit
introducing Bitcoin in Kibera's Soweto village since 2022. What started
as a waste-management program became an earn, save and spend loop: about
2,600 community members onboarded (Feb 2026), merchant counts reported
between 40+ and 150+ (sources disagree; treat the range as reported,
not verified), 3.78 million sats described as earned, spent and
circulated locally (no time window given, so treat as a point claim),
11 local projects supported, and a goods marketplace at
kibera.marketplace.afribit.africa moving buyer-to-artisan-group
payments in bitcoin.

Sources: [Inclusive Fintech Forum](https://www.inclusivefintechforum.com/media-hub/latest-news/lessons-a-kenyan-village-transacting-in-bitcoin-harbours-in-inclusive-fintech?hs_amp=true),
[Blink brief 2026/36](https://www.blink.sv/blog/weekly-brief-2026-36),
[Blink brief 2026/10 waste program](https://www.blink.sv/blog/weekly-brief-2026-10),
[Tech-ish on Kibera merchants](https://tech-ish.com/2026/04/14/adopting-bitcoin-nairobi-2026-kenya-crypto/),
[Afribit proof pack (PDF)](https://abacusai-apps-4267ca06bfdf80c3582a4474-us-west-2.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/27744/public/marketing/afribit-bitcoin-circular-economy-proof-pack.pdf).

**Bitcoin Ekasi (Mossel Bay, South Africa).** The earn loop stated
plainly: "Learn. Earn. Spend. Repeat." Extension of The Surfer Kids
youth non-profit (since 2010, bitcoin since 2021): about 20 staff paid
100% of salaries in bitcoin, around 30 shops onboarded, kids earning
weekly sats in the diploma program and spending at local merchants over
Lightning (Bolt cards, Blink addresses), a walk-in support centre, a
home-painting program paying 7,000 sats weekly.

Sources: [Lightning.news on Ekasi](https://lightning.news/bitcoin-ekasi-transforming-a-forgotten-township/),
[Blink brief 2026/09](https://www.blink.sv/blog/weekly-brief-2026-09),
[Adopting Bitcoin Cape Town on Ekasi](https://www.bitcoinafricastory.com/news/adopting-bitcoin-cape-town-2027-south-africas-bitcoin-circular-economy-season),
[Ekasi staff and merchants](https://www.bitcoin-builder.com/blog/these-are-10-projects-that-are-transforming-africas-face-thanks-to-bitcoin/).

**Others in Africa.** A December 2025 summit gathered 15+ circular
economy projects from nine countries (Kenya: AfriBit Kibera,
BitBiashara, Bitcoin Chama). BitBiashara runs a Tena (Nairobi) hub with
merchants; merchant counts not re-verified here. Sats4Safari runs
bitcoin tourism. Bitcoin Dada educates African women (HRF grantee).

Source: [Africa circular economies summit](https://www.bitcoinafricastory.com/news/africa-bitcoin-circular-economies-mauritius-el-zonte).

**The pattern that worked:** pay locals in bitcoin for real work,
onboard shops they already use, keep the loop local, stay small and
trusted. Kibera's goods marketplace is the nearest proof that a
listing-style tool fits a Kenyan circular economy.

### What failed, and the lessons

**Chivo (El Salvador).** Top-down beats grassroots at downloading an
app and loses at everything else. NBER (1,800 households): only 20.6%
of downloaders kept using Chivo after the $30 bonus; 60% never
transacted again; FUSADES: 97.75% of firms never processed one bitcoin
sale; 88% of accepting businesses converted to dollars at once.
Technical failures, identity theft, frozen funds. January 2025 reform:
acceptance voluntary, wallet being wound down. Lesson: bonuses buy
downloads, not users; mandates buy resistance; custody and trust
failures are fatal.

Sources: [Chivo four years later](https://www.satsintel.io/en/blog/chivo-wallet-el-salvador-four-years-later),
[NBER and FUSADES figures](https://scenarica.substack.com/p/the-reversal),
[88% converted at once](https://www.blockready.com/blog/el-salvador-bitcoin-experiment),
[IMF reform](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/08/26/bitcoin-payments-el-salvador-decline/).

**Dependence on one rail.** Prior Altradits research notes Bitika's
week-long Sep-Oct 2026 outage through a mobile-money partner (reported
in Blink brief 2026/41; not re-verified here). Lesson: any single
partner can go dark; the Kazi board therefore names no partner in its
pay path (poster and worker settle however they agree).

**Custody without a licence.** Kenya's VASP transition ended 4 Nov
2026; custodial wallets face wallet-provider licensing. See the full
analysis on the site's [research page](/research). Lesson: the new app
holds nothing, which is a hard constraint, not a preference.

### Kenyan services to build beside (not on)

**Tando (bitcoin.co.ke).** Lightning to M-Pesa: launched July 2024,
100+ transactions a day by mid-2025; since 12 May 2026 every M-Pesa
number (40 million) works as a Lightning address; unclaimed numbers
convert to KES at 1%. kTown already rides this API for merchant
receive. Kazi needs no Tando integration: a worker paid in sats can
spend at kTown shops or cash out however they choose.

Sources: [Blink on 40M addresses](https://www.blink.sv/blog/40-million-kenyans-now-have-a-bitcoin-lightning-address),
[Tando taxi, steak and coffee](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/tando-enables-bitcoin-payments-for-taxi-steak-and-coffee-in-kenya-via-lightning-network),
[Kenya's Lightning leap](https://bitcoinnews.africa/kenyas-lightning-leap-tando-turns-40-million-m-pesa-numbers-into-bitcoin-addresses/).

**Bitika (bitika.xyz).** M-Pesa and Airtel to sats, paid to the
buyer's own Lightning wallet, no sign-up. Minimum reported as KSh 10
in some sources and KSh 50 in others (discrepancy noted; could not
verify the live figure). 3% fee per the site's research page. For Kazi
it is the top-up path: a poster who runs dry buys more sats the same
way any Kenyan does. No integration.

Sources: [Lightning.news Bitika profile](https://lightning.news/bitika-disrupting-finance/),
[Bitika from KSh 10](https://www.btcbreakdown.com/p/issue-474),
[Bitika how it works](https://onsandoabucheri.medium.com/a-bitcoin-breakthrough-how-bitika-is-making-bitcoin-accessible-to-every-kenyan-ebe44b3fe67e).

**Others named in prior Altradits research** (Machankura USSD wallet,
Blink, Fedi, Kotani Pay, BTCPay, LNbits) stay as context; see
[research](/research). None is a dependency of this app.

## 4. The choice: Kazi board

### The problem (one)

Kisumu has no earn-first path into sats. Buying first sats with
scarce shillings competes with free M-Pesa Kadogo transfers, and the
kTown pilot shops will open with no local customers holding sats.
Worked examples all start with earn: El Zonte cleanups, Kibera waste
collection, Ekasi salaries and class rewards. Kisumu needs its own
earn path before it needs more spend tools.

### The app (one)

**Kazi board at /kazi.** A moderated noticeboard of small paid tasks
for Kisumu people.

- Anyone posts a task: title, what to do, reward (KES or sats, as
  typed), where in Kisumu, one contact line, poster name.
- Nothing goes public until Stanley approves it (pilot: a glance at
  each task, like pinning a note on a market board).
- A Kisumu worker answers through the posted contact, does the task,
  and the poster pays them directly, peer to peer: sats to the
  worker's own Lightning wallet, or M-Pesa, as they agree.
- The poster closes the task when done (manage link shown once at
  posting). Tasks expire after 30 days. Visitors can flag a bad task;
  flags go to Stanley, nothing auto-hides (no griefing).
- Caps: 20 open tasks (KAZI_MAX_OPEN), pilot wording throughout,
  "small tasks only".
- Altradits holds no money, takes no cut, converts nothing, sees no
  payment. The pay path names no partner, so no partner outage can
  break it.

### Why this fits Kisumu

- **Adoption:** earn beats buy. A student who earns 2,000 sats
  translating a page holds sats with no shillings risked, then spends
  them at a kTown pilot shop. That is the Ekasi loop in Kisumu form.
- **Life:** income plus skills for youth and students facing 16.8%
  unemployment at 20 to 24. Digital tasks (translation, transcription,
  design, testing) suit campus life; physical tasks (photos, errands,
  painting, cleaning) suit estates and markets.
- **Growth:** money flows INTO Kisumu. Diaspora and remote bitcoiners
  post tasks (plot photos, Kiswahili checks, local research) and pay
  Kisumu workers. Local shops post too (a kTown shop paying sats for
  a painted sign recycles sats inside the town).
- **Seedable:** Stanley is the first buyer. He posts and pays the
  first small tasks himself (Kiswahili review of this very board,
  photos of pilot shops, market price checks), so the board opens
  with real paid work, not an empty page.
- **Honest:** nothing promised that is not live. Pilot wording, real
  tasks only, no invented users or volumes, ever.

### Why not the alternatives

- **Bring-your-own-wallet till for sats-keeping shops:** needs shops
  that keep sats and customers holding sats. Both are absent in
  Kisumu today, and onboarding the same shops would split Stanley's
  time with the kTown pilot. Revisit when Kazi earners need spend
  options beyond kTown.
- **Chama multisig coordinator:** biggest cultural fit, wrong first
  build. PSBT group flows and group-key support load are too much
  for one dev, and a beginner's key mistake risks real savings.
- **Fish or market trade board:** real Kisumu need, decorative
  bitcoin link. Fishermen and traders want KES today; that board
  would be a fiat marketplace wearing a sats sticker.
- **Merchant directory or map:** empty at launch, thin afterwards.
  Listings of places to spend can join /kazi later if earners ask.
- **Learn-and-earn lessons:** pays from Stanley's own pocket and
  smells like Chivo bonuses. Real tasks pay from real buyers.
- **Anything custodial:** blocked by the licence question. See
  /research.

## 5. Build notes (for the PR)

- One page: kazi.html at root (own path /kazi), EN/SW word list in
  the page script, task content stays in the poster's language.
- Small API: api/kazi.js + lib/kazi.js under a KAZI=on switch (off
  by default, health always answers). Routes: list open tasks, post
  a task (pending), manage with token (edit, close), flag a task.
- Moderation: admin actions (pending list, approve, hide) plus a
  compact admin view. Nothing public before approval.
- Security: rate limits on every route, input cleaning, no stored
  HTML (render with textContent), manage tokens stored hashed,
  30-day expiry, pilot caps, tests in test/.
- Wiring: server.mjs PUBLIC + route, .htaccess rule, sitemap.xml,
  page CSP, frame check, report.js, portfolio card with a real
  screenshot.
- Data, minimum: title, description, reward, location, poster name,
  contact line (shown on the listing, that is its purpose), manage
  token hash, timestamps, terms version. No accounts, no emails
  unless the poster types one as their contact, no payment data
  (payments happen off the board). Deletion on request via /contact.
- Kiswahili: simple UI wording written against kTown's word list;
  MUST be reviewed by a native speaker (Stanley) before launch. If
  any string is doubted, that string ships in English only.
- Open decisions for Stanley: pilot task cap (20 proposed), whether
  diaspora-posted tasks are welcome from day one, the KAZI_HELP
  contact line, and the lawyer questions below.

## 6. Lawyer questions (no legal advice given)

1. A moderated noticeboard where strangers arrange paid tasks and
   settle peer to peer, with Altradits holding nothing: any VASP,
   employment-agency, or other licensing angle?
2. Does hand-approving each task change liability versus unmoderated
   listings? Is the approve-before-public design the safer one?
3. Does the answer change if a flat posting fee or a featured-task
   fee is added later? (No fees at launch.)
4. ODPC: register, or exempt? Poster names and contact lines are
   published by design; what must the terms and privacy notice say?
5. Consumer and worker protection: what safety wording must the
   board carry (meet in public, agree payment before work, small
   tasks, no advances)?
6. Records: what to keep and for how long (closed tasks, flags,
   moderation actions)? Any retention ceiling?
7. Company form for the pilot (sole vs limited): what changes for
   liability and tax?

## 7. Grow and stop signals (30 days, 20 open tasks)

Grow if: 15+ real tasks posted, half filled with workers paid (as
reported by posters on closing), repeat posters, support under 30
minutes a day, one task paid in sats and spent at a kTown shop.
Stop or rethink if: tasks go unanswered, posters will not pay or
workers report no pay twice, flag abuse eats days, support passes 1
hour a day, or legal advice says wait. If stopped, the board freezes
as read-only history; nobody is stranded, since no money ever sits
on it.
