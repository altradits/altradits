      1. Your node and the blockchain

      See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro getinfo

      Your node is two programs. Bitcoin Core downloads and checks every block. LND runs Lightning on top of it.

      A new block arrives about every 10 minutes. Your node must keep up, or it can't safely pay or get paid.

      Your node, its peer and the network

      See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro getnetworkinfo · ln-ro listpeers

      Your node has a public key: its address on the Lightning Network. It learns the network graph (every public node and channel) from its peers, and uses it to find routes.

      Peers

      Ping is how long a message takes to the peer and back. Reconnections count every drop since the peer was first seen, so a big total can be old. A peer flagged as dropping had a drop in the last day, or 5 or more drops a day: payments can fail during a drop.

      2. Channels: where your Lightning money sits

      See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro listchannels

      A channel is Bitcoin locked between you and one peer. The total never changes. Paying moves sats to their side. Getting paid moves sats to your side.

      Each side keeps about 1% it can't spend: the reserve. It lets a cheater be punished.

      Liquidity, plainly

      See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro channelbalance · ln-ro listchannels

      Every channel is a seesaw: your side is what you can send, the peer's side is what you can receive. The total never moves. Each payment just shifts sats from one side to the other.

      Receiving needs room: inbound is all peer sides added up, minus the 1% reserves. A top-up or a buy fills your side (more to send, less room to receive). A send does the reverse.

      A lopsided channel needs no closing: nearly all yours → spend or send out to reopen receiving room; nearly all theirs → receive in (a top-up) to rebuild sending power. Close and reopen only when a peer is dead or the channel is tiny.

      Inside each channel

      See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro listchannels · ln-ro closedchannels

      A channel starts as one on-chain transaction (the funding transaction). Its short channel ID says where: block × transaction × output. Every payment through it is an update to a shared balance sheet both sides sign.

        3. Sending power and top-ups

        To send, sats must be on your side. A top-up is an invoice from your own node that you pay from another wallet. The sats cross the channel to your side.

        1. Pick an amount: a preset, the suggested figure (covers every Ye₿ente balance plus a buffer), or your own between the shown min and max.
        2. Make the invoice, then pay it within the hour from any Lightning wallet: scan the QR, copy it, or press Open in wallet. If the fee looks huge, read Fees below before you confirm.
        3. Watch this page: it flips to “Paid ✓” with your new balance, and the numbers reload. Sending power rises by the amount; receiving room shrinks by the same amount.
        4. If it expires unpaid, nothing moved. Just make a new one.
        5. If the button errors or max shows 0, receiving room is empty: a top-up arrives over Lightning, so it needs inbound first. Open a channel with inbound (Channels tab advice), then retry.

        On the Mac: verify, deposit, withdraw

        Verify this top-up landed. ln-ro listinvoices --max_invoices 5 shows your recent invoices with the memo “Altradits node top-up”; paid ones read SETTLED. ln-ro channelbalance shows your side grew by the amount.

        Deposit more. Press the button again, or run lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet addinvoice --value <sats> and pay the invoice from another wallet. A top-up arrives over Lightning, so it needs receiving room first, which is why the button has a max.

        Withdraw. The dashboard can never spend: the spending key stays on the Mac. Over Lightning, get an invoice from the wallet you are sending to, then lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet payinvoice <invoice>. On-chain, lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet sendcoins <address> <sats> --sat_per_vbyte <fee>, then wait for confirmations.

        In between. Lightning top-ups settle in seconds with no confirmations to wait for, and the paying wallet covers a small routing fee. You can never send the locked 1% reserve or more than “Can send now”. If an invoice expires unpaid, nothing moved: just make a new one. Keep the Mac awake with LND running until a payment lands. Every command above, plus more, sits in Learn → Verify.

        Fees: why a payment can look expensive

        Your node charges nothing to receive. The fee you see is quoted by the wallet you pay from, and it stays there: none of it reaches your node.

        Why it can look huge. A first use often opens a channel, which is an on-chain transaction priced in sat/vB and taken from the amount. An exchange withdrawal often carries a flat fee that hurts small amounts most. A swap-based wallet wraps on-chain cost plus its own cut. And a very large amount over thin routes simply costs more to carry.

        Pay less. Top up from a wallet that already holds Lightning balance, not from an exchange. Split a big top-up into smaller invoices: each finds cheaper routes. Do any first-time channel open while the slow fee is low. Always read the fee your wallet quotes before you confirm: cancel and split the amount if it looks wrong. Failed attempts cost nothing: a payment settles or fails, never half.

        Is paying now urgent? Look at the top of this panel. If it says “The node can't send anything right now” or “Short:”, topping up is urgent: Ye₿ente withdrawals fail until you do. Otherwise it can wait.

        Best time to pay. The Lightning part costs about the same at any hour: nights and weekends change nothing. Anything touching the chain (a first channel open, a swap, an on-chain send) is cheapest when the slow fee is low: single digits in sat/vB is cheap, and the current price sits under “Fee now” in the On-chain panel. If it is not urgent, wait for a cheap-fee moment.

        Fund it, step by step

        1. On the Mac, make an address: open Terminal (no cd needed, run from anywhere) and enter lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet newaddress p2wkh. It prints a bc1q address. Making stays on the Mac, so this server holds no key that touches addresses: there is nothing here to steal that can make them or spend them.

        2. Paste it below and press Show QR code. The dashboard only turns your pasted text into a code. It never makes addresses and never moves money.

        3. Send bitcoin to it from an exchange or another wallet, scanning the code or copying the address.

        4. Wait for 1 confirmation (about 10 minutes), then press Check again. The coin appears above, and the balance moves.

        Verify: ln-ro walletbalance shows the sats, ln-ro listunspent shows the coin, and each coin above links to its mempool.space page. If a Terminal window does not know ln-ro yet, the one-line setup is in Learn → Verify.

        Stay safe: compare the first and last 6 characters with your Mac's screen before sending, since the screen is the truth. Use each address once, and only make one when you are about to fund (the wallet watches only the newest 20 unused ones). First time with a new address, send a tiny test amount and confirm it arrives before the rest. Never trust an address pasted from a chat or email.

        If you liquidate: getting your sats back

        Verify on the Mac: ln-ro pendingchannels · ln-ro walletbalance (see Learn → Verify)

        Liquidating means closing every channel and pulling all sats back into the on-chain wallet, then to a wallet you alone hold. It takes days, not minutes.

        1. Cover Ye₿ente wallets first. Those balances are users' money, owed in real sats. Let users withdraw (or pay them out) before you touch channels.
        2. Close each channel cooperatively: lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet closechannel <channel_point>. Your side arrives on-chain after a few blocks. Find each ID with the “close ID” row in Learn → Verify.
        3. Stuck or offline peer? Add --force. It costs more, and your funds wait out a delay of up to about two weeks before they unlock.
        4. Watch the closes land. Track them with ln-ro pendingchannels; the on-chain balance grows as each one confirms.
        5. Sweep everything to your own wallet: lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet sendcoins --sweepall <YOUR_ADDRESS> --sat_per_vbyte <FEE>. Check the fee rate on mempool.space first, and double-check the address: on-chain sends can't be undone.
        6. Verify zero. ln-ro listchannels and ln-ro pendingchannels show nothing left, and ln-ro walletbalance holds everything.
        • Never delete the seed or channel.backup until every sat is confirmed on-chain.
        • Keep the node online until the last close confirms, so it sees every closing transaction.
        • Closing when fees are high costs more. If nothing is urgent, wait for a quiet mempool.

        Your wallet address, and how to use it

        Verify on the Mac: lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet newaddress p2wkh · ln-ro listunspent

        Your node makes a new address every time you ask. Each one starts with bc1q. Use each address once: reusing one lets anyone link your payments together.

        Only make an address when you are about to fund: every address shown burns a fresh one, and the wallet watches only the newest 20 unused ones.

        1. Make one on the Mac: lncli --lnddir=$HOME/.lnd-altradits --network=mainnet newaddress p2wkh, then paste it in the Fund it box above for a QR code.
        2. Use it to fund the on-chain wallet from an exchange or another wallet, or to receive any on-chain payment.
        3. Verify it: compare the first and last 6 characters on the Mac's own screen. Never trust an address pasted from a chat or email. After the sender pays, the coin appears in the list above and in ln-ro listunspent.
        • Don't generate dozens of addresses without using them: past about 20 unused ones the wallet can stop seeing them (the “gap limit”).
        • An invoice (lnbc…) is for Lightning; an address (bc1q…) is for on-chain. They are not interchangeable.

        5. Routing and fees

        See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro feereport

        Nodes pass payments for each other and charge a small fee: a base fee per payment plus a rate in parts per million (ppm).

        Example: base 1 sat + 1 ppm on 100,000 sats = 1.1 sats.

        Your channel is private. Others can pay you through it, but it won't route strangers' payments. That's normal for a shop or wallet node.

        Payments and invoices, last 30 days

        See it yourself on the Mac: ln-ro listpayments · ln-ro listinvoices

        Payments are what your node sent; each paid a small routing fee to the nodes on the way. Invoices are requests your node made; settled ones were paid, cancelled ones expired unpaid.

        Money received through the website

        Verify on the Mac: the “Money received” row in Learn → Verify (set ADMIN_TOKEN from the server's settings first). Each payment also links its receipt.

        Every payment the website took, in sats: orders and donations before the agency moved out, and support payments since. Ye₿ente deposits aren't here; they belong to wallet holders.

        Your wallets

        How a custodial wallet works: the sats sit on your node. A wallet is a record of how many of them belong to someone.

        Receiving makes an invoice on your node for that wallet. When it's paid, the node has more sats and the wallet's record goes up by the same amount.

        Sending pays from the node and takes the amount (plus the real routing fee) off the record. It always needs the wallet's PIN on a trusted phone, so it happens in the Ye₿ente app, never with this admin token.

        Try it from the command line, and watch the dashboard change

        Receive 21 sats into your wallet step by step, and see where each step shows up. Uses the ln-ro shortcut from Learn → Verify. If curl is refused (403), open the same address in the browser.

        1. Ask your Lightning address what it accepts:
          curl -s https://altradits.com/.well-known/lnurlp/HANDLE | jq '{callback, minSendable, maxSendable}'
          Amounts are in millisats: 1 sat = 1,000 msat.
        2. Ask the callback for an invoice of 21 sats (21,000 msat):
          curl -s "CALLBACK?amount=21000" | jq -r .pr
          Dashboard: Ye₿ente → Open invoices goes up by 1.
        3. Read the invoice your node made:
          ln-ro decodepayreq INVOICE | jq '{destination, num_satoshis, expiry}'
          The destination is your node's public key (Node & network tab).
        4. Pay it from another wallet (your phone). Then check the node:
          ln-ro listinvoices --max_invoices 1 | jq '.invoices[0] | {state, amt_paid_sat}'
          State becomes SETTLED.
        5. Within 2 minutes, refresh this page: the wallet's balance goes up by 21 sats, Owed to wallets rises, and ln-ro channelbalance shows 21 more on your side.

        6. Ye₿ente and how payments reach the node

        Ye₿ente is custodial: wallet balances are sats your node owes people. Can send must stay above Owed to wallets.

        How a payment arrives:

        1. A wallet looks up name@altradits.com at /.well-known/lnurlp/name (LNURL-pay).
        2. It asks for an amount. Your node makes an invoice for exactly that, tied to the request by a hash.
        3. The payer's wallet finds a route to you and pays. Your node holds the payment until it's settled.
        4. Every 2 minutes the website checks open invoices and credits the wallet that was paid.

        Most active Ye₿ente wallets

        Verify on the Mac: the “Most active wallets” row in Learn → Verify (set ADMIN_TOKEN from the server's settings first).

        Deposits and completed transactions per wallet, most active first. Use it to reward your most active users, for example with a transfer from your own Ye₿ente wallet.
